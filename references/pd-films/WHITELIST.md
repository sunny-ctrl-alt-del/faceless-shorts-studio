# Classic Cuts: which films we may use (owner chose public-domain film edits, 2026-11-10)

## The rule
A film may be used only if it is public domain in **both** the US and India (India shown; use the owner's country):
- **US**: published 1930 or earlier (public domain since 1 Jan 2026; 1931 films join on 1 Jan 2027), OR a later film
  whose copyright lapsed (no notice / not renewed) confirmed by 2+ independent sources.
- **India**: films are protected 60 years from the year after publication, so published **1965 or earlier**
  (1966 films join on 1 Jan 2027).
- Use an old transfer from the Internet Archive (`https://archive.org/download/...`), not a new restoration with a new
  score or added material. We replace the soundtrack with our own music; only short dialogue clips keep the film audio.
- Never: films with trademarked characters (Superman, Mickey Mouse, Popeye…), anything still sold as a current
  restoration with new music, colourised versions, or any film that fails either test. Unsure → don't use it.
- Caveats to remember: a script or novel behind a film can have its own longer term (e.g. Remarque's novel in India
  until 2031); YouTube Content ID sometimes claims public-domain footage by mistake (dispute with the film's year and
  public-domain status).

Silent films: set `"mute": true` on dialogue clips (intertitle text becomes the captions; never keep a transfer's score).

## Open movies under Creative Commons Attribution (modern look, free to reuse commercially WITH credit)
Blender Studio open movies (studio.blender.org; files on download.blender.org): reuse allowed worldwide under CC BY,
so the US/India public-domain test doesn't apply; the **credit is mandatory** in the description, e.g.
"Film: Sintel (2010) © Blender Foundation | durian.blender.org · CC BY 3.0". Logos are excluded from the licence (crop
them out). Tears of Steel's *soundtrack* is CC BY-ND, so never keep its music (we replace music anyway). Content ID
sometimes claims these by mistake: dispute with the licence link.
